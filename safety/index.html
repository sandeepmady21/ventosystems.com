Safety Systems

Location, alerting and response for resorts, campuses and work sites. We start with one area and one response team, and measure the result before anything grows.

Pilot program

Plan a pilot Pilot options

How it works

The sequence a pilot tests from end to end.

  1. 01SignalA pass, wristband, phone, camera or sensor records an event.
  2. 02Location and timeThe event is recorded with where and when it happened.
  3. 03AcknowledgmentThe responsible member of staff is alerted and acknowledges it.
  4. 04OutcomeThe outcome is recorded after investigation, so the response can be reviewed.

Where it fits

Places where staff need to find someone quickly when something goes wrong, and know what to do next.

Resorts and ski areas

Guests spread across slopes, lifts and lodges, with patrol and guest services who need a last known location fast.

Campuses

Buildings, open spaces and events, with security teams who need alerts tied to a place and a clear way to respond.

Access points and work sites

Gates, entrances and site perimeters, where a pass or check-in can record a last known location.

Pilot options

Starting points for scoping. Coverage, technology and measures are agreed for the chosen use case.

01

Location and check-in

How a pass, wristband or phone could provide a last recorded location or check-in, with agreed coverage, update frequency and access.

02

Signal detection

Camera or sensor inputs tested against a defined event, comparing alerts with what actually happened under site conditions.

03

Alerts and response

The full sequence from signal to acknowledgment to recorded outcome, with missed signals and delays measured.

04

Equipment monitoring

One equipment condition or fault pattern: which signal is useful, when staff should investigate and what action follows.

What we measure

Every pilot reports against the same measures, agreed before it starts.

Coverage
Where the system can locate people and detect events.
Missed events
Real events the system did not flag.
False alarms
Alerts that did not match a real event.
Acknowledgment time
How long staff take to respond to an alert.

People decide

The system prepares the information. Your staff make every call.

Your rules

Your team sets what should raise an alert, who receives it and how quickly it must be acknowledged.

Your response

Every dispatch and every follow-up is a decision by a person. Alerts carry the evidence they are based on.

Your existing plans

It adds to patrols, sweeps and emergency procedures, and does not replace them. Equipment monitoring flags a condition for your team; it does not operate equipment or replace inspections.

Data and privacy

Before a pilot starts, we agree with you how guests and staff take part, who can view site data and how long it is kept. The system runs in the environment you choose, and only the roles you name can see its data.

  • How people take part, agreed before launch
  • Access limited to the roles you name
  • Retention period set in advance
  • Runs where the operator chooses

How a pilot runs

A first stage produces a pilot scope, a test plan and the evidence to decide what comes next.

  1. 01DefineChoose one area, event and response team, and map the inputs and decisions involved.
  2. 02TestRun the whole response and measure coverage, missed events, false alarms and acknowledgment time.
  3. 03ReviewCompare results with the agreed measures and decide whether a wider trial is justified.
