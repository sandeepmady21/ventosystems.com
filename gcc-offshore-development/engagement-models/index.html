What each model looks like

01

Team extension

Add a specialist to a team that already has an engineering lead, a backlog, and a way of shipping. A data engineer could take on an integration, or a QA engineer could expand test coverage alongside your existing team.

Your leads define priorities, review work, and approve releases. Scope the role, how candidates are assessed, and who handles day-to-day support and staffing changes.

Discuss team extension ↗
02

Dedicated engineering teams

Form a team around one ongoing product, platform, or workstream. A squad might combine software engineers, QA, and a technical lead, with design or data roles where the work calls for them.

Your product owner sets the roadmap and acceptance criteria. Agree who leads engineering day to day, how the squad works with other teams, and which reviews or releases need client approval.

Discuss a dedicated team ↗
03

Offshore development center (ODC)

Build an India-based engineering operation for sustained product development or R&D. Start with a defined domain, such as enterprise integrations, and plan how engineering, quality, and platform roles can grow around it.

Define the center’s engineering mandate and reporting line. Scope local leadership, recruiting, employment, facilities, and IT operations. Assign each responsibility to your team, Vento, or an agreed local partner.

Discuss an offshore development center ↗
04

Global capability center (GCC)

Plan an India center with a long-term mandate tied to your wider business. Product engineering may be the first function, with room for data, platform, or other functions as the mandate grows.

Your leadership defines how the center fits into the business. Work through which functions launch first, who leads them, and which decisions sit with the center. Agree how legal, employment, workplace, and other operating needs will be handled.

Discuss GCC planning ↗
05

Build–Operate–Transfer (BOT)

Use a staged setup when the goal is to take over the team or center. The parties agree who builds and operates it first, what the client will take on, and what has to be ready before handover.

Define the transfer conditions early. They may cover staffing, agreements, equipment, repositories, access, documentation, and support after handover. Availability and terms depend on the structure and the parties involved.

What to decide first

Define the first mandate

Name the work the team should own and who will review it. A skills gap, a product backlog, and a center mandate each call for a different starting scope.

Assign operating responsibilities

Agree who leads the work, approves hires, employs the team, and manages local operations. Record those responsibilities alongside the commercial terms.

Set ownership expectations

Discuss code, IP, and access during scoping. If the plan includes taking over a team or center, set the conditions for transferring it and name the receiving owner.
