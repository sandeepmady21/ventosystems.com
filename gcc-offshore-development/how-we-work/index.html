From discovery to delivery

The depth of each stage depends on the model. Adding a specialist to an existing team calls for a narrower setup than establishing a center.

01

Agree on the first scope

Review the product or workflow, current systems, constraints, and the people who make decisions. Pick an initial workstream and a milestone someone on your side can review.

Document the expected outcomes, first roles, reporting lines, decision owners, and operating responsibilities. For a center, include the local leadership and support the first function needs.

02

Assess engineers against the work

Define each role against real tasks the team will face. The assessment can include a work sample and a technical conversation covering trade-offs, testing, or how the candidate handles unfamiliar systems.

Agree who sources and evaluates candidates, where your team joins interviews, and who approves each hire. Confirm who will employ each person and provide day-to-day support.

03

Integrate with your engineering setup

Onboard the team into your issue tracker, repositories, review process, and release workflow. Agree identity and access controls, where code and data can be handled, and who can approve changes or access to production.

Use the first piece of work to test the full path from ticket and code review to acceptance. Record any gaps in access, documentation, or ownership while they’re still easy to resolve.

04

Review delivery as work progresses

Agree a review cadence and a named contact on each side. Reviews cover what shipped, quality issues, blockers, and decisions that need attention, alongside the next priorities.

Choose measures that fit the mandate. For a platform team that might mean release reliability and defect trends. For an integration team, it could mean error rates and the time it takes to resolve failed records.

05

Run the team and expand where needed

Use delivery reviews to decide whether the mandate calls for additional roles, a second workstream, or more local leadership. Review capacity, onboarding, documentation, and support before expanding the operation.

For a GCC, agree how each additional function fits into the parent company’s reporting, decisions, and operating cadence.

06

Plan transfer if ownership is the goal

For Build–Operate–Transfer, agree the receiving owner, what can transfer, and what readiness looks like before setup. The handover plan can cover people and agreements, equipment, access, documentation, and operational records.

Agree who signs off on each stage, how access and support will change, and what post-handover assistance is in scope.

What we agree before setup

Engineering decisions

Name the people who own the roadmap, approve candidates, review architecture, and sign off on delivery. Set expectations for reporting and escalation.

Code, data, and IP

Record where repositories and environments live, who can access them, who owns the work product, and how existing IP, third-party dependencies, and credentials are handled.

Local operations

Assign responsibility for recruiting, employment, payroll, compliance, equipment, and workspace. Agree which responsibilities sit with the client, Vento, or local providers and how those costs are covered.

What a first milestone can show

Choose an outcome the client can inspect, then use it to decide how the team should work or grow.

A working integration

Connect one source system end to end, with sample records, tests, and a way to review exceptions. Use the result to check system access, ownership, and delivery handoffs.
